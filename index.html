Every AI answer starts at a power plant.

The same question can cost very different amounts of energy and carbon. It depends on which model answers, which chip runs it, which grid powers it, what hour it is, and the device you watch it on. This page lets you play with all of them.

The equation
CO₂e = energy × PUE × carbon intensity
+ embodied carbon

Energy depends on the model, chip and workload. PUE is data center overhead. Carbon intensity depends on place and hour. Embodied is the carbon of making the chips, buildings and your device.

01

The lineage: from power plant to your screen, and back

Every prompt pulls energy through seven steps. Two grids are usually involved: the one powering the data center and the one powering your own device.

01GenerationHydro, nuclear, wind, solar, gas, coal. The mix sets how much CO₂ each kWh carries.
02 · THE MOVERGridCarbon intensity (g CO₂/kWh) changes by region and by hour as sun, wind and demand move.
03Data centerCooling and power losses add overhead (PUE). Cooling can also use water.
04HardwareCPUs, GPUs, TPUs. Work per watt sets the kWh per answer. Making chips has its own carbon.
05AI modelBigger models and long agent sessions burn more tokens. Same task: over 40× energy spread between models.
06NetworkMoving text across the internet. Small for chat, but it adds up with files and images.
07 · OFTEN FORGOTTENYour deviceThe laptop or desktop you watch on. Running a model locally moves all the work here, on your home grid.
↺ and back

Clean demand funds new wind, solar and storage through power purchase agreements.

Carbon-aware scheduling moves flexible jobs to clean hours and clean regions.

Heat reuse, longer device life and recycling shrink the embodied part.

What this means
    Sources

    Grid intensity: Ember Yearly Electricity Data, 2025 annual national averages, via GreenCalculus grid emission factors.

    Cloud model energy and embodied carbon per session: CLEER Dashboard by Sustainable AI Group (CLEER-Text-0926, data CC BY-NC 4.0). Capability index: Artificial Analysis, as listed in CLEER.

    Prices: Claude API pricing, Claude plans, GitHub Copilot plans and Copilot usage-based billing. Other models: list prices via CLEER / Artificial Analysis.

    Data center chips: vendor peak dense BF16 specs and rated power. Device manufacturing: Apple product environmental reports and the laptop-co2e compilation, rounded; desktops estimated.

    Assumptions (read before quoting numbers)

    Hourly grid curves are an illustrative model calibrated to annual averages, not live data. Average, not marginal, intensity is used. Data center PUE per region is assumed. Device power, memory bandwidth and compute are typical estimates. Local speed is derived from them, assuming 4-bit weights and 75% of agent context reused from cache. Session token counts are back-calculated from CLEER's per-session costs and list prices (they match published prices exactly for Claude and GPT-5 models). Costs: list API prices, cache reads at 10% of input (25% for GPT-4.1, 50% for GPT-4o), Anthropic cache writes at 1.25×; batch discounts and enterprise deals are not included. Claude plan prices from claude.com/pricing; Copilot plans and AI credits from GitHub (usage-based billing since June 2026). Desk electricity at $0.30/kWh. Week = 5, month ≈ 18.3, year = 220 workdays. Network energy uses 0.03 kWh per GB. Device manufacturing is spread over 4 years × 1,760 work hours. Rooftop solar is counted at 41 g/kWh lifecycle and assumed to cover daytime use in proportion to sunshine. All Azure regions shown use CET, except UK South (one hour behind).
